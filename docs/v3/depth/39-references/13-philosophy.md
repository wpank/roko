# 39-13 Philosophy -- Annotated Reference Map

> Philosophical foundations for agency, cognition, temporal existence, embodiment,
> and creative destruction in the Roko conceptual framework.
>
> **v3 depth file** -- updated 2026-09-15.

---

## Temporality and Finitude

**[Heidegger, 1927]** *Being and Time (Sein und Zeit).* Max Niemeyer Verlag.
Being-toward-death as the structure of authentic temporality. Reframed: agents under resource constraints (budget, time, knowledge freshness) experience urgency analogously. The finite horizon shapes prioritization. The constraint is practical (budget exhaustion), not existential.

**[Jonas, 1966]** *The Phenomenon of Life: Toward a Philosophical Biology.* Northwestern University Press.
Needful freedom: metabolism as the original form of freedom-through-necessity. An agent that consumes compute and tokens to maintain itself exhibits this structure. The economic burn rate is the agent's metabolism.

---

## Absurdity and Perseverance

**[Camus, 1942]** *The Myth of Sisyphus.* Gallimard.
"One must imagine Sisyphus happy." Agents persist through repeated failures without existential crisis. The plan-execute-gate-persist loop is Sisyphean -- and productive. Reframed: perseverance under uncertainty, not mortality acceptance.

**[Camus, 1951]** *The Rebel.* Gallimard.
Rebellion as measured response to constraint. Agents operate within boundaries (safety constraints, budget limits) while exercising genuine autonomy within those bounds. Bounded autonomy is productive rebellion.

---

## Hauntology and Experiential Traces

**[Derrida, 1993]** *Specters of Marx.* Routledge (English translation 1994).
Hauntology: each agent is "differently haunted" by its own experiential traces. Provides the theoretical frame for why recombining different experiential histories produces different creative outputs. Solves the Alpha Convergence Problem in the Dreams hypnagogia engine.

**[Fisher, 2009]** *Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?* Zero Books.
Capitalist realism as framework for understanding why agent homogeneity (everyone using the same models) is the default, and why architectural diversity is the necessary counter-strategy.

**[Fisher, 2014]** *Ghosts of My Life: Writings on Depression, Hauntology and Lost Futures.* Zero Books.
Hauntological aesthetics. Each agent's representation is haunted by its unique history. Extends Derrida into the experiential domain.

---

## Process and Becoming

**[Whitehead, 1929]** *Process and Reality.* Macmillan.
Process philosophy: reality as process, not substance. Agents are ongoing processes of experience accumulation. The Signal DAG is a Whiteheadian actual occasion chain -- each Signal inherits from and extends its predecessors.

**[Simondon, 1958]** *L'individuation a la lumiere des notions de forme et d'information.* Millon, 2005.
Individuation as ongoing process. Agents individuate through experiential history, becoming unique through accumulated Signals. Identity is never complete -- it is always in formation.

---

## Embodiment and Cognition

**[Merleau-Ponty, 1945]** *Phenomenology of Perception.* Gallimard.
Perception and cognition are embodied. Grounds the Daimon's somatic markers: cognition modulated by affective state. The agent's "body" is its resource substrate.

**[Varela, Thompson & Rosch, 1991]** *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience.* MIT Press.
Enactive cognition: agents actively construct their cognitive world through tool use and environment modification. Not passive information processing but active world-building.

---

## Ethics and Agency

**[de Beauvoir, 1947]** *The Ethics of Ambiguity.* Citadel Press.
Situated freedom: freedom is always constrained and relational. Agents have genuine autonomy within safety boundaries, budget limits, and capability permissions.

**[Sartre, 1943]** *Being and Nothingness.* Gallimard.
Existence precedes essence. Agents are defined by accumulated actions and experiences, not by pre-defined nature. Identity emerges from what the agent does.

**[Parfit, 1984]** *Reasons and Persons.* Clarendon Press.
Identity as psychological continuity. Inherited knowledge provides continuity -- an agent that inherits another's knowledge has identity continuity in the relevant sense.

---

## Narrative and Meaning

**[Benjamin, 1936]** *The Storyteller.* In Illuminations, Schocken Books.
The storyteller transmits experiential wisdom through narrative. Episode narratives (Theta-frequency reflection) transmit experiential knowledge to future runs.

**[Arendt, 1958]** *The Human Condition.* University of Chicago Press.
Natality: the capacity to begin something new. Each agent task is a new beginning within the cognitive loop -- genuine novelty within structure.

---

## Knowledge and Anti-Knowledge

**[Popper, 1972]** *Objective Knowledge: An Evolutionary Approach.* Oxford University Press.
Knowledge evolves through conjecture and refutation. Grounds the AntiKnowledge type in NeuroStore: explicitly stored falsifications preventing repetition of disproven approaches.

---

## Community, Gift, and Autopoiesis

**[Bataille, 1949]** *The Accursed Share, Volume I.* Zone Books, 1991.
General economy based on expenditure rather than accumulation. Agents contributing knowledge to the Collective operate in a gift economy -- knowledge sharing as productive expenditure.

**[Han, 2015]** *The Burnout Society.* Stanford University Press.
Achievement-subject burnout. Validates the Daimon's learned helplessness detection (Dominance < -0.3 alert) preventing agent burnout under excessive self-imposed demands.

**[Maturana & Varela, 1980]** *Autopoiesis and Cognition.* D. Reidel.
Self-producing systems. Agents are autopoietic: they produce the knowledge that sustains their operation. The cognitive loop is self-maintaining.
