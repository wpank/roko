# 39-15 Cybernetics and VSM -- Annotated Reference Map

> Research foundations for feedback-based control, the Viable System Model, and
> cybernetic regulation as they apply to Roko's cognitive architecture.
>
> **v3 depth file** -- updated 2026-09-15.

---

## First-Order Cybernetics

**[Wiener, 1948]** *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine.* MIT Press.
Foundational cybernetic feedback-based control. Roko's cognitive loop is cybernetic: sense-compute-act-verify with feedback at every step.

**[Ashby, 1956]** *An Introduction to Cybernetics.* Chapman & Hall.
Law of Requisite Variety: the controller must have at least as much variety as the system it regulates. Determines the minimum model complexity in the CascadeRouter -- simple tasks need simple models.

**[Conant & Ashby, 1970]** *Every Good Regulator of a System Must Be a Model of That System.* International Journal of Systems Science, 1(2), 89--97.
World model requirement: agents must maintain domain models. Why NeuroStore is architectural, not optional -- the regulator (agent) must model its domain.

**[Maxwell, 1868]** *On Governors.* Proceedings of the Royal Society.
First mathematical analysis of feedback control (steam engine governor). Conceptual ancestor of all cybernetic regulation in Roko. The Gate pipeline is a governor.

---

## Viable System Model

**[Beer, 1972]** *The Brain of the Firm.* Allen Lane.
Five-system recursive VSM. Roko mapping: S1 = agent execution (Cells), S2 = pheromone field (coordination), S3 = Gate + budget (operational control), S4 = NeuroStore + HDC (intelligence), S5 = config + policy (identity and ethos).

**[Beer, 1984]** *The Viable System Model: Its Provenance, Development, Methodology and Pathology.* J. Operational Research Society, 35(1), 7--25.
VSM diagnostic framework for identifying organizational dysfunction. Maps to Roko's `roko doctor` diagnostics and health monitoring.

---

## Autopoiesis and Self-Production

**[Maturana & Varela, 1980]** *Autopoiesis and Cognition.* D. Reidel.
Self-producing systems. Agents produce the knowledge that sustains their operation -- the cognitive loop is autopoietic. Cross-referenced in [13-philosophy](./13-philosophy.md).

**[Varela, 1991]** *Organism: A Meshwork of Selfless Selves.* In Organism and the Origins of Self, Springer.
Network selfhood. The Collective is a meshwork of autonomous agents, each self-organizing yet constituting a coherent whole.

---

## OODA and Decision Loops

**[Boyd, 1987]** *Patterns of Conflict.* Unpublished.
OODA loop: Observe-Orient-Decide-Act. Roko's 9-step cognitive loop extends OODA with verification (Gate) and learning (NeuroStore update). The additional steps are what makes Roko self-improving rather than merely reactive.

---

## Perceptual Control Theory

**[Powers, 1973]** *Behavior: The Control of Perception.* Aldine.
Behavior controls perception, not output. Agents act to bring perceived state into alignment with reference (goals). Grounds goal-directed behavior in the CorticalState energy fields.

---

## Predictive Regulation

**[Sterling, 2012]** *Allostasis: A Model of Predictive Regulation.* Physiology & Behavior.
Anticipatory regulation vs. reactive homeostasis. Roko's predictive foraging system anticipates knowledge needs rather than reacting to gaps. Grounds the T0 probe's predictive context assembly.

**[Cannon, 1932]** *The Wisdom of the Body.* W.W. Norton.
Homeostasis: maintaining internal stability. The simplest form of the regulatory principle operating at multiple timescales -- tick-level (T0), task-level (Theta), and session-level (Delta).

---

## Multi-Level Learning

**[Argyris & Schon, 1978]** *Organizational Learning.* Addison-Wesley.
Single-loop (fix errors), double-loop (change strategy), triple-loop (change the learning process). Maps directly to Roko's Gamma (reactive execution), Theta (strategy reflection), Delta (consolidation and meta-learning).

**[Tosey, Visser & Saunders, 2012]** *The Origins and Conceptualizations of Triple-Loop Learning.* Management Learning, 43(3), 291--307.
Critical review of triple-loop learning providing nuanced understanding of when each loop level activates.

---

## Second-Order Cybernetics

**[Von Foerster, 1979]** *Cybernetics of Cybernetics.* In Communication and Control in Society, Gordon and Breach.
Second-order cybernetics: the observer is part of the system. Roko's meta-cognition is second-order -- the agent reasons about its own reasoning process via Theta-frequency reflection.

**[Bateson, 1972]** *Steps to an Ecology of Mind.* Ballantine.
Levels of learning and logical types. Multi-level adaptation where learning at one level changes the rules for learning at another. Grounds the distinction between operational learning and meta-learning.

---

## Adaptive Systems

**[Lo, 2004]** *The Adaptive Markets Hypothesis.* Journal of Portfolio Management, 30(5), 15--29.
Markets are adaptively, not perfectly, efficient. Agents must continuously adapt rather than converging to a fixed strategy. Validates the CascadeRouter's continuous learning.

**[Soros, 1987]** *The Alchemy of Finance.* Simon & Schuster.
Reflexivity: predictions alter what they predict. Grounds contrarian retrieval (15% mandate) and calibration awareness -- agents must account for how their actions change the environment.
