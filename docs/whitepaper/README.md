## Thesis

Golden path plus cybernetics. A frontier model plans; cheap models execute granular tasks in parallel on a DAG,
in worktrees; gates and a whole-plan check verify the result; and feedback loops make the system improve
measurably. The paper describes this ideal state and tags every mechanism with its status at a commit, so that a
reader can tell what runs today from what is only designed. Much of it is only designed so far, and the
cheap-model half of the thesis is untested on real work: every portal task ran on one pinned model (N3 under
"Canonical numbers").

## Claims and sources

Each section's claims, with an id, a kind, the writer's source and what the published text cites. Each stub
repeats its own rows. A writer may reword or drop a claim, and may add one only with a source; record the change
here in a small commit of its own.

**Kinds:**
- `design`: what Roko is designed to do, in the ideal-state voice, with its status tag in the same sentence or
  table row.
- `status`: a tag at a commit, taken from the status matrix, never from the tldr.
- `number`: a count, amount, rate or ratio. It needs a footnote with its source and window; shared numbers are
  listed under "Canonical numbers".
- `lit`: a result from the literature. It needs a `[@key]` from `references.bib`, and the setting it was measured
  in.
- `case`: a field case, quoted from a frozen file in `evidence/`.
- `scope`: a limit or hedge the section must state.
- `figure`: a figure the section introduces.

"Writer's source" uses the short names under "Sources". Most of those inputs are gitignored, so the published
text never cites them: it cites what the last column says. A key in backticks there (`chen2024frugalgpt`) is not
yet in `references.bib`; the section that first cites it adds it after checking it with refcheck.

### §2 Design principles (`02-design-principles.md`, gap-370d3c)

| Id | Kind | Claim | Writer's source | Cite in the text as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DP1 | lit | Size tasks for the executor, not for cohesion: models' 80%-success horizons are 4–6× shorter than their 50% horizons, and per-step errors compound. | tldr/04 rule 1; C1 | [@kwa2025measuring]; [@sinha2025illusion] |
| DP2 | lit | Split on independent outputs, not sequential steps: parallel agents helped decomposable work (+80.8%) and hurt sequential work (−70%). | tldr/04 rule 2; C1 | [@kim2025towards] |
| DP3 | lit | The planner writes the gating checks and the implementer never does: model-written oracles tend to encode the code's actual behaviour, not the expected one. | tldr/04 rule 3; C2 | [@konstantinou2024do] |

| DP7 | lit | Put ambiguity back into authoring: models don't notice underspecification, and a check before execution recovers most of the loss. | tldr/04 rule 7; C2 | [@vijayvargiya2025ambigswe]; [@edwards2026askorassume] |

## Conventions

### Status header

Line 1 of every section file (`NN-*.md` and `appendix-*.md`) is its status header:

    Status: stub · budget 550 words · owner gap-353d57

- **State:** `stub` (this outline's placeholder), `draft` (written and lint-clean) or `reviewed` (set by the
  review, gap-8d2c79). `paperlint --strict` fails on `stub`, and the epic's exit check requires `reviewed`.
- **Budget:** the number in the Outline table. `--strict` fails outside 0.5–1.3× of it. The count covers the words
  after the header, tables and captions included, and leaves out fenced blocks and footnote definitions. The
  appendix's header says `budget none`.
- **Owner:** the work item that writes the file.
- `build.sh` strips the header from the PDF.

### Voice and tense

- The ideal-state voice with honest tags: describe the design, and put its status tag in the same sentence or
  table row.
- §0, §1, §8 and §10, the parts people quote, say "is designed to" for anything that is not WIRED (W9).
- Never claim an effect without evidence. A result that doesn't exist yet is called pending, with the item that
  will produce it.
- Give the setting of every literature result (single function, QA, documents or repository scale), so that no
  result reads as broader than it is.
- Numbers carry denominators. Use plain mechanism names; the banned words are listed below.

### Status tags

- Write a tag as `TAG@<sha>`, e.g. `PARTIAL@a17d4dadd`: the tag in capitals, `@`, and a short sha of at least 7 hex
  digits (use the 9 that `git log --oneline` prints). The commit must be an ancestor of HEAD.
- Take tags from the status matrix at its pinned commit, never from the tldr: the tldr is tagged at `d9e79e9d8`,
  and some of its rows have moved since (see "Sources").
- Every mechanism that is not WIRED also names the item or epic that would change its tag.
- The vocabulary is tldr/00's:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| WIRED | Runs on the production path (`roko plan run`, `roko run`) and does its job: a sensor records, a regulator changes behaviour |
| PARTIAL | On the path, but incomplete, masked or fed wrong data |
| ORPHANED | Worked under Runner-v2 (deleted 2026-09-06 in `6b5da8616`) and was not re-attached |
| BUILT-UNWIRED | Code and tests exist, with no production caller |
| DOCS-ONLY | `docs/` describes it as working, but there is no meaningful code |
| MISSING | Not built; it may already be specified |
| REMOVED | Deliberately deleted |
| BROKEN | Wired, but does the wrong thing |
| UNPROVEN | Might be true; no measurement exists |

### Markers

The research draft's grammar (`paper/00-README.md`). Markers belong in stubs and drafts in progress:
`paperlint --strict` fails on any `[[…]]` left outside code, so a finished section has none.

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[[TODO: …]]` | Work still to do |
| `[[CITE?: …]]` | A claim still missing its source; never ship one |
| `[[AS-BUILT: …]]` | Design text still to check against the code |
| `[[RESULT …: …]]` | A result that must come from an experiment. A finished section replaces it with a sentence saying the result is pending, and naming the item that will produce it |

TOML table names such as `[[task.verify]]` inside code spans are not markers.

### Citations and sources

- **Literature:** `[@key]`, with the key in `references.bib`. Check each new key with
  `tmp/cybernetic-harness/tools/refcheck.py` before adding it, and add keys in a small commit of their own.
- **Vendor pages:** an `@online` entry with its `urldate`. A vendor's numbers stay the vendor's claims.
- **The repository:** tracked files and commits only. Write a repo path from the root in backticks
  (`crates/roko-graph/src/engine.rs`) and its symbol as a separate code span (`plan_to_graph`); avoid line
  numbers, which drift. Write a commit as a 9-digit short sha in backticks. Cite a work item by its id, e.g.
  spec-6ac537.
- **Gitignored inputs** (the tldr, the research notes, the programme specs S01–S11, the field evidence and the
  research draft) are never cited directly. Cite the tracked epic or item that carries them, or freeze the part
  you quote into `evidence/`.

### Numbers and footnotes

- Every paragraph or table with a `$` amount, a percentage, an `N×` ratio or an "N of M" count has a footnote
  naming its source: a commit, a frozen file in `evidence/` with its key, a `[@key]`, or a work item. The footnote
  also gives the window or scope.
- Footnote labels start with the section number, because `build.sh` joins the files: `[^7-portal]`.
- For example:

      Roko built most of the portal: 16 plans and 173 tasks for $174.87 of recorded agent spend.[^7-portal]

      [^7-portal]: `evidence/<date>-<slug>.json` (sha256 `<first 12 hex digits>`), keys `…`. Portal plans only,
          attempts to 2026-09-29 07:41Z; the supervising sessions are excluded.

### Frozen evidence

Anything quoted from a gitignored source is copied into `evidence/` and fixed there by its hash. gap-29a64e (§7)
creates the directory; other sections add files in small commits of their own.

- **Only derived, scrubbed data:** counts, rollups, tables and case write-ups. Never transcripts, prompts,
  environment dumps, or anything that could hold a key (bug-7d7200).
- **Names:** `evidence/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>.<ext>`, dated by when the source generated it.
- **Hashes:** `evidence/SHA256SUMS` holds one `shasum -a 256` line per file. Check them with
  `cd docs/whitepaper/evidence && shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS`.
- **Provenance:** `evidence/README.md` has one row per file: its source path, the tool and time that generated it,
  the commit it describes, its window, and what was removed from it.
- **Never edit a frozen file.** To refresh one, freeze a new dated file and update the footnotes that cite it.
- A footnote cites the file, the first 12 hex digits of its sha256, and the key or row it quotes.

### Code identifiers

- Every backticked identifier or repo path must exist at HEAD (`git grep -w`, `git ls-files`).
- A designed name that doesn't exist yet is followed by `(designed)`, or its sentence or table row carries a
  `MISSING@<sha>` tag. W9 found eight such names in the research draft's §4 (`AttemptKey` and `cost_source` among
  them): don't carry them over unmarked.
- docs/v3 is not evidence: its headline claims were checked against the code, and several failed (AR9).

### Figures

- SVG files in `figures/`, named `figN-<slug>.svg`: `fig1-architecture.svg` (§3), `fig2-golden-path.svg` (§4)
  and `fig3-status-matrix.svg` (§9). gap-d1d92c draws them.
- Until then a section carries the caption ("Figure N: …") and a text diagram in a fenced block.
- Include a figure as `![Figure N: caption](figures/figN-<slug>.svg)`. Local links and figure paths must resolve.

### Headings

`# N Title` for a section and `## N.M Title` below it (the abstract is `# Abstract`). `build.sh` doesn't renumber.
